User guide

How to use Requel 2.0, a web application for writing requirements with everyone who has a stake in them. To install and run it, see Installing and running Requel.

How Requel thinks about requirements

Everything lives in a project. A project holds the requirements, the people who work on them, and the discussion about them.

Goals
What the system must achieve, and why. Goals can support, refine, conflict with or depend on each other.
Stories
A short narrative of one person using the system, either succeeding or hitting a problem.
Actors
The roles that use the system: people, other systems or devices.
Use cases and scenarios
A use case says what an actor does with the system. Its scenarios are the steps, as a main flow plus alternatives and exceptions.
Glossary
The project's terms and what they mean, with alternate names pointing at the preferred one.
Stakeholders
The people and groups with a say in the requirements. Users who are stakeholders get permissions on the project.
Annotations
Notes and issues attached to any of the above. An issue collects positions (possible resolutions) and arguments for and against them until someone resolves it.
Assistants
Automated checks that read the text and raise issues: vague words, spelling, complex sentences and likely glossary terms, plus the optional AI review.
